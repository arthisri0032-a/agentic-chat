01 Executive Summary

This project evaluates the privacy and data protection posture of a hypothetical consumer health and fitness mobile application (“the App”) that collects biometric, location, and behavioral data from users and wearable devices. The assessment applies recognized frameworks — the NIST Privacy Framework, ISO/IEC 27701, and core principles from GDPR, CCPA, and HIPAA-adjacent health-data handling — to identify data protection gaps and propose a practical remediation framework.

Six material risks were identified, the most severe relating to unencrypted biometric data transmission, overly broad consent language, and indefinite data retention. A phased control framework is recommended to bring the App's data handling practices in line with regulatory expectations and industry best practice.

02 Scope & Objectives

The assessment covers the App's end-to-end personal data lifecycle: collection (in-app sensors, wearable sync, manual entry), transmission, storage, processing (including third-party analytics), and deletion.

  • Map all categories of personal and sensitive (biometric/health) data collected and processed
  • Assess privacy and security risk across the data lifecycle using a standard risk-rating methodology
  • Evaluate current consent and disclosure practices against GDPR/CCPA requirements
  • Recommend a prioritized data protection control framework

03 Methodology

The assessment followed a four-stage approach aligned to the NIST Privacy Framework (Identify → Govern → Control → Communicate) and ISO/IEC 27701 privacy information management guidance:

  • Data mapping — inventory of data categories, sources, storage locations, and retention periods
  • Risk identification — structured walkthrough of the data lifecycle to identify control gaps
  • Risk rating — likelihood × impact scoring to prioritize findings
  • Control recommendation — mapping each finding to a specific, actionable mitigation

06 Key Findings

Biometric and health data are the App's highest-sensitivity assets and require handling controls beyond those applied to general account data.
Consent mechanisms do not currently meet the specificity and granularity expected under GDPR Art. 9 (special category data) or CCPA sensitive personal information provisions.
Third-party SDKs (analytics, crash reporting) can become an unintended path for sensitive data to leave the App's control boundary if payloads are not minimized.
No formal data retention or deletion schedule exists for biometric data, creating unnecessary exposure.

07 Recommended Data Protection Framework

7.1 Governance

  • Designate a privacy owner responsible for DPIAs on new features touching health or location data
  • Maintain a living data inventory/record of processing activities (ROPA)

7.2 Technical Controls

  • Encrypt sensitive fields (biometric, location) at the application layer, in addition to transport-layer TLS
  • Apply data minimization before data reaches third-party SDKs
  • Implement automated retention enforcement (scheduled anonymization/deletion jobs)

7.3 Privacy by Design

  • Redesign consent UX to capture granular, purpose-specific permissions with plain-language explanations
  • Build a self-service DSAR workflow with audit logging

7.4 Third-Party Risk Management

  • Require a signed Data Processing Agreement (DPA) and privacy/security questionnaire before onboarding any vendor that touches personal data

08 Conclusion

Applying a structured privacy risk assessment methodology to the App surfaced concrete, prioritized gaps rather than generic recommendations — from encryption of specific data fields to the redesign of consent flows. The proposed framework gives the organization a phased path to reduce residual risk across all six identified findings while building the governance foundation needed to support ongoing compliance as the App's data use evolves.
